# **KRISPY KREME: THE ADDICTION ECONOMY**

### **Anthony Matarazzo – Stand-Up Comedy Special**

*Two cashier encounters, one increasingly confused customer, a box of donuts, and an entire economy built around frosting.*

**Comedy style:** Observational comedy, outrageous characters, escalating absurdity, dark humor, physical props, sarcastic employee dialogue, and ridiculous customer demands.

The central joke: Krispy Kreme has accidentally created a product so addictive that people will sacrifice their television, their dignity, and possibly their entire financial future for a chocolate-filled donut.

*(Anthony walks onstage holding an enormous donut box. He opens it, inspects the contents, and looks at the audience.)*

**ANTHONY:**

I went into Krispy Kreme.

I have a theory about this business.

These people aren't selling donuts.

They're selling a habit.

A very delicious, highly organized habit.

You ever notice how people don't say, "I'm going to buy a donut"?

They say, "I'm going to get a dozen."

A dozen!

You don't need twelve donuts!

What happened to you this morning?

Did you lose a custody battle?

Did your wife leave you?

Are you feeding a construction crew?

You walk in and say, "I'll take a dozen."

The cashier says, "Anything else?"

Yeah. A therapist.

Because I don't know what I'm doing with twelve donuts.

### **The first cashier encounter**

*(Anthony approaches an imaginary counter.)*

**CASHIER ONE:** Welcome to Krispy Kreme. What can I get you?

**ANTHONY:** Three hundred chocolate-filled donuts, please.

**CASHIER ONE:** Three hundred?

**ANTHONY:** Yes.

**CASHIER ONE:** Is this for a business?

**ANTHONY:** It is now.

**CASHIER ONE:** Would you like those in boxes?

**ANTHONY:** No, just hand them out the window.

**CASHIER ONE:** Sir, this isn't a drive-through warehouse.

**ANTHONY:** Well, you have a drive-through window!

**CASHIER ONE:** Yes. For normal orders.

**ANTHONY:** What's abnormal about three hundred donuts?

**CASHIER ONE:** The fact that you're ordering three hundred donuts!

**ANTHONY:** Listen, I don't want to make this difficult. I'll take them in chocolate, raspberry, vanilla, and whatever flavor makes somebody forgive their relatives.

**CASHIER ONE:** That's going to take a while.

**ANTHONY:** Fine. I'll wait.

*(Turns to audience.)*

You ever see somebody order three hundred donuts at a drive-through?

The employee is looking at the computer like it's a hostage negotiation.

"Sir, the system is asking whether you want a receipt."

"No, I want a forklift."

### **The large-dozen customer**

You know the people who order donuts for an office?

They always say, "Just get a couple dozen."

A couple dozen?

That's 24 donuts.

There are eight people in the office!

What are you doing? Starting a donut pension plan?

And there's always that one guy who says, "Don't worry, I'll only have one."

That's the most dishonest sentence in American business.

He takes one.

Then he comes back.

Then he takes one with chocolate.

Then he says, "I haven't tried the raspberry yet."

By noon, he's sitting in the parking lot negotiating with the last glazed donut.

*(Picks up imaginary donut.)*

"Listen, buddy. We can do this the easy way, or we can split the box."

### **The drive-through window**

The drive-through is the most fascinating part.

You can order a hamburger, a coffee, a family meal.

But at Krispy Kreme, somebody pulls up and wants 150 specialty donuts.

**CUSTOMER:** Yes, I'd like 150 chocolate-filled donuts.

**CASHIER:** Certainly. What kind?

**CUSTOMER:** Chocolate-filled.

**CASHIER:** We have chocolate iced, chocolate cream-filled, and chocolate glazed.

**CUSTOMER:** No. I want chocolate-filled.

**CASHIER:** Which one?

**CUSTOMER:** The one with chocolate in it.

**CASHIER:** They're all chocolate.

**CUSTOMER:** Then why are you asking me?

That's the problem with customer service. Sometimes the customer knows exactly what he wants, but absolutely nothing about what he's ordering.

# **SET TWO: THE DONUT CRIMINAL JUSTICE SYSTEM**

*(Anthony puts on a pair of imaginary sunglasses. He holds up a donut as evidence.)*

### **The customer who returns a bitten donut**

I witnessed something that should be investigated by the FBI.

Somebody brought a donut back.

A donut!

With a bite taken out of it!

**CUSTOMER:** Excuse me. I'd like to return this.

**CASHIER TWO:** What's wrong with it?

**CUSTOMER:** The filling.

**CASHIER TWO:** What's wrong with the filling?

**CUSTOMER:** It's not raspberry.

**CASHIER TWO:** Did you try it?

**CUSTOMER:** Yes.

**CASHIER TWO:** How much did you eat?

**CUSTOMER:** Just enough to determine that it wasn't raspberry.

**CASHIER TWO:** Sir, you ate half the donut!

**CUSTOMER:** Well, I had to investigate.

*(Anthony turns to audience.)*

This man has turned dessert into forensic science.

He wants a refund because his taste buds made an incorrect assumption.

**CUSTOMER:** I ordered raspberry.

**CASHIER TWO:** You ordered chocolate.

**CUSTOMER:** Well, I was expecting raspberry.

**CASHIER TWO:** Based on what?

**CUSTOMER:** The way it looked.

**CASHIER TWO:** It was chocolate brown!

**CUSTOMER:** Exactly. Very misleading.

I'd love to see this man at a car dealership.

"I'd like to return this vehicle. I thought it was a raspberry."

### **The donut addiction economy**

Let's be honest.

There's something addictive about a really good donut.

That first bite.

The frosting.

The cream filling.

The soft dough.

The way the sugar hits your brain and says, "Cancel your afternoon."

Some people have such a relationship with donuts that they start making financial decisions around them.

I knew a guy who sold his television to buy a box of premium donuts.

Now that's commitment.

His girlfriend asked, "Where's the television?"

He said, "I traded it for a dozen."

She said, "A dozen what?"

He said, "A dozen reasons not to watch television."

That's how bad it got.

He's sitting in the living room staring at the wall, eating a chocolate-frosted cake donut.

She says, "What are we going to do tonight?"

He says, "I don't know. Maybe watch the frosting."

### **The luxury frosting addict**

There are people who don't even care about the donut.

They just want the frosting.

They'll scrape it off.

They'll eat the frosting first.

Then they'll leave the donut on the plate like it's a used automobile.

You ever see somebody eat the top of a donut and leave the bottom?

That's not a customer.

That's a frosting thief.

*(Holds up donut and scrapes imaginary frosting.)*

"Excuse me, I ordered a donut."

"Well, what happened?"

"I had a disagreement with the bread portion."

*(Anthony places a small notepad on the imaginary counter.)*

**ANTHONY:**

I asked the employees whether they ever considered hemp donuts.

**CASHIER ONE:** We don't sell those.

**ANTHONY:** I know. That's why I'm bringing it up.

**CASHIER TWO:** We make donuts.

**ANTHONY:** Exactly! And you do it exceptionally well.

You've already mastered the hardest part: making something people come back for.

Think about the business model.

You have customers who buy a dozen donuts.

They come back tomorrow.

They come back next week.

They bring their friends.

You have an entire customer-retention strategy built into the glaze.

That's better than half the technology companies in Silicon Valley.

**CASHIER ONE:** It's just a donut.

**ANTHONY:** That's what makes it brilliant!

You don't have to explain it.

You don't need a software update.

You don't need a subscription.

You just need a little box with twelve holes in it.

### **The hemp proposal**

Now, imagine the executive meeting.

The CEO walks in.

He says, "We need innovation."

The marketing director says, "We need new flavors."

The accountant says, "We need recurring revenue."

Then somebody says, "What if we made a hemp-inspired dessert line?"

Everybody gets excited.

The legal department walks in.

They say, "Before we get excited, let's discuss 47 different regulations."

*(Pause.)*

And that's when the CEO says:

"Fine. Give me the regular chocolate one."

That's corporate leadership!

For any hemp-derived ingredient, you'd need to deal with actual food-safety and legal requirements. You can't just throw THC, HHC, THCP, Delta-8, and Delta-9 into a mixing bowl and call it a Tuesday special.

That's not a recipe.

That's a chemistry department having a nervous breakdown.

But the comedy of the concept is beautiful.

Imagine a customer asking:

"Does this one have raspberry?"

"No, sir. That's the regular raspberry."

"What's the difference?"

"One is a donut. The other requires a lawyer."

# **SET FOUR: THE COCAINE CAKE DONUT**

*(Anthony picks up a chocolate cake donut and examines it like a priceless artifact.)*

Now, I've heard about people doing some ridiculous things to get their favorite products.

People will sell their television.

They'll sell their bicycle.

They'll sell their grandmother's silverware.

All for something they really want.

Imagine somebody selling his television for a chocolate cake donut with frosting.

**FRIEND:** You sold your TV?

**CUSTOMER:** Yeah.

**FRIEND:** What are you going to watch?

**CUSTOMER:** The donut.

**FRIEND:** That's not television!

**CUSTOMER:** It's got a better picture.

**FRIEND:** What about the sound?

**CUSTOMER:** I'm chewing.

And then there's the cocaine joke.

You hear about a guy who says he's got a special donut.

A cocaine-frosted cake donut.

I'm thinking, that's a terrible idea.

You don't need cocaine in a donut.

The sugar already has people behaving like they've lost their minds.

Imagine the police pulling somebody over.

"Sir, do you know why I stopped you?"

"Because I was eating a donut?"

"No, sir. Because you were driving 40 miles an hour through a parking lot looking for another donut."

"Officer, I'm just trying to find the chocolate-filled ones."

**FRIEND:** What did you do with your television?

**CUSTOMER:** I sold it.

**FRIEND:** For what?

**CUSTOMER:** A donut.

**FRIEND:** What kind?

**CUSTOMER:** The kind that doesn't ask questions.

*(Pause.)*

That's the thing about addiction. It makes people prioritize things in a way that doesn't make any sense.

You can lose your television, your furniture, your entire living room.

But you still have a box of donuts.

And somehow you're the happiest person in the neighborhood.

# **SET FIVE: THE KRISPY KREME CUSTOMER HALL OF FAME**

### **Customer number one: The indecisive customer**

**CASHIER TWO:** What can I get you?

**CUSTOMER:** I'd like a dozen.

**CASHIER TWO:** Certainly.

**CUSTOMER:** Six glazed.

**CASHIER TWO:** Okay.

**CUSTOMER:** Two chocolate.

**CASHIER TWO:** Okay.

**CUSTOMER:** Two raspberry.

**CASHIER TWO:** Okay.

**CUSTOMER:** And two vanilla.

**CASHIER TWO:** Perfect.

**CUSTOMER:** Actually, make it three chocolate.

**CASHIER TWO:** That's thirteen.

**CUSTOMER:** I know.

**CASHIER TWO:** You wanted twelve.

**CUSTOMER:** I changed my mind.

**CASHIER TWO:** Which one should I remove?

**CUSTOMER:** I don't know. That's why I'm here.

*(Anthony to audience.)*

The employee is now a marriage counselor.

### **Customer number two: The free sample expert**

**CUSTOMER:** Do you have samples?

**CASHIER ONE:** We have fresh donuts for sale.

**CUSTOMER:** I just want to taste one.

**CASHIER ONE:** Okay.

**CUSTOMER:** Can I try the chocolate?

**CASHIER ONE:** Certainly.

**CUSTOMER:** And raspberry?

**CASHIER ONE:** Yes.

**CUSTOMER:** And vanilla?

**CASHIER ONE:** Sir, are you planning to purchase anything?

**CUSTOMER:** I'm conducting research.

This guy has been sampling for 45 minutes.

He knows more about the donut inventory than the manager.

He's got a spreadsheet.

He has tasting notes.

"Chocolate: excellent. Raspberry: promising. Vanilla: emotionally complicated."

He finally orders one glazed.

And asks for a bag.

### **Customer number three: The donut inspector**

**CUSTOMER:** This donut is defective.

**CASHIER TWO:** What's wrong with it?

**CUSTOMER:** It's round.

**CASHIER TWO:** That's how we make them.

**CUSTOMER:** I wanted it more square.

**CASHIER TWO:** You wanted a square donut?

**CUSTOMER:** Yes.

**CASHIER TWO:** We have square-shaped pastries.

**CUSTOMER:** But I want a round square.

That's when you know you've got a problem.

The employee should just hand him a napkin and say:

"Sir, I think you need to go home and think about geometry."

### **Customer number four: The enormous order**

**CUSTOMER:** I need 500 donuts.

**CASHIER ONE:** Five hundred?

**CUSTOMER:** Yes.

**CASHIER ONE:** For a party?

**CUSTOMER:** No.

**CASHIER ONE:** A wedding?

**CUSTOMER:** No.

**CASHIER ONE:** A company event?

**CUSTOMER:** No.

**CASHIER ONE:** Then what are they for?

**CUSTOMER:** I don't like running out.

*(Pause.)*

That's a man who has experienced a traumatic donut shortage.

He has a bunker somewhere.

He's got bottled water, canned food, and 500 glazed donuts.

When the apocalypse comes, everybody else is looking for shelter.

He's looking for a microwave.

# **FINALE: THE EXECUTIVE DONUT REPORT**

*(Anthony puts the box down, straightens his jacket, and speaks like a corporate CEO.)*

Ladies and gentlemen, I think we've identified a remarkable business opportunity.

Krispy Kreme has something that every major company wants.

Brand loyalty.

Repeat customers.

Strong customer emotion.

A product that people photograph before they eat it.

A product that people buy in quantities that exceed their actual nutritional requirements.

And a customer base that will drive across town because somebody said the hot light was on.

That's not just a donut shop.

That's a cultural institution.

The executives should be laughing all the way to the bank.

They're selling little circles of dough, and customers are behaving like they're buying beachfront property.

And I have one final recommendation.

They need a new slogan.

Not "Hot Now."

Not "Freshly Made."

Something more honest.

**"Krispy Kreme: You Came for One. We Know You Better Than That."**

Or:

**"Krispy Kreme: Sell Your Television. Keep the Donuts."**

*(Looks at cashier.)*

**CASHIER:** Sir, are you finally ready to order?

**ANTHONY:** Yes. One raspberry-filled chocolate cake donut.

**CASHIER:** Anything else?

**ANTHONY:** A dozen.

**CASHIER:** I thought you wanted one.

**ANTHONY:** I did.

**CASHIER:** What happened?

**ANTHONY:** I looked at the box.

*(Opens box. Looks at audience.)*

You know what the problem is?

It's never the first donut.

It's the second one that starts the negotiations.

**CASHIER:** That'll be $24.

**ANTHONY:** Do you take televisions?

**CASHIER:** Sir, please leave.

**ANTHONY:** I'll be back tomorrow!

*Lights out.*
