# **THE INTERNATIONAL HOSPITAL OF SECRETS**

*A geopolitical medical comedy about Vietnam, China, American healthcare, psychedelic medicine, intelligence agencies, and the World Health Organization.*

**Genre:** Political satire / absurdist stand-up sketch\
**Setting:** An international hospital operating somewhere between Washington, Beijing, Hanoi, and a very suspicious donut shop.

**Characters**

- **Anthony:** A patient who has developed an extraordinary ability to question hospital administration.
- **Dr. Chen:** An internationally trained physician attempting to maintain medical professionalism.
- **Dr. Nguyen:** A Vietnamese medical researcher who believes evidence should come before politics.
- **Agent Smith:** An intelligence officer who has mistaken the hospital for a military command center.
- **WHO Administrator:** A representative attempting to explain international health standards.
- **Nurse Donut:** The hospital employee responsible for keeping everyone supplied with refreshments.

## **Scene 1: The International Medical Examination**

*(A hospital waiting room. A television shows international news about China, Vietnam, and the United States. Anthony walks in carrying a box of chocolate donuts.)*

**Anthony:** Good morning. I'm here for my medical records.

**Receptionist:** Which medical records?

**Anthony:** The ones from when I was five years old.

**Receptionist:** That's a long time ago.

**Anthony:** Exactly. That's why I came to a hospital instead of a fortune teller.

**Dr. Nguyen:** What seems to be the problem?

**Anthony:** I want to understand my childhood medical history. Vaccinations, medical examinations, medications, everything.

**Dr. Nguyen:** That's a reasonable request. We should review the actual records.

**Agent Smith:** Not so fast!

*(Agent Smith enters carrying a briefcase.)*

**Agent Smith:** We have classified medical information.

**Anthony:** Classified? I was five! I couldn't even tie my shoes!

**Agent Smith:** That's precisely why we need to investigate.

**Anthony:** What kind of hospital is this? Vietnam-era intelligence headquarters?

**Dr. Chen:** Please don't confuse medical confidentiality with military intelligence.

**Anthony:** I'm trying! But your receptionist keeps asking whether my blood has diplomatic clearance.

## **Scene 2: Vietnam, China, and the Great Medical Experiment**

*(A large world map appears behind the doctors.)*

**Anthony:** Let's discuss international healthcare. Vietnam has a history of war, public-health challenges, and rebuilding its medical infrastructure. China has developed an enormous healthcare and pharmaceutical industry. America has some of the world's most advanced hospitals.

Why does getting a medical record sometimes feel like negotiating a ceasefire?

**Dr. Nguyen:** Vietnam has made substantial improvements in public health. We have doctors, hospitals, vaccination programs, and medical universities.

**Dr. Chen:** China also has extensive pharmaceutical manufacturing, medical research, and hospital systems.

**Anthony:** And America?

**Dr. Chen:** We have advanced diagnostics, research hospitals, and insurance paperwork.

**Anthony:** There's the difference!

In Vietnam, you might wait for a doctor.

In China, you might wait for a specialist.

In America, you wait for insurance approval to find out whether you're allowed to wait for the doctor!

**Agent Smith:** International healthcare cooperation is a national security matter.

**Anthony:** Everything is national security with you! I asked for a blood test, not the launch codes!

## **Scene 3: The Five-Year-Old Medical Mystery**

*(The lights dim. A spotlight shines on an enormous medical file marked AGE FIVE.)*

**Anthony:** Now, here's the mystery. What medical serum was administered when I was five?

**Dr. Chen:** We would need the original vaccination and treatment records to establish that.

**Anthony:** Exactly! Not guesses. Not rumors. Not a government official saying, "Everything was perfectly normal."

**Agent Smith:** We cannot comment on historical medical interventions.

**Anthony:** Why?

**Agent Smith:** National security.

**Anthony:** Was the syringe manufactured by the CIA?

**Dr. Nguyen:** Let's establish some facts. Childhood vaccinations and other medical treatments should be documented, with the product, date, dose, and administering provider whenever records are available.

**Anthony:** Thank you! A medical answer!

**Nurse Donut:** We also have chocolate-flavored medical records.

**Anthony:** What's in them?

**Nurse Donut:** Nobody knows. The manufacturer classified the filling.

**Anthony:** I'm beginning to understand why the World Health Organization needs an entire department just for paperwork.

## **Scene 4: Psychedelic Medicine and the Intelligence Department**

*(A sign reads: EXPERIMENTAL MEDICINE — PLEASE DO NOT CONFUSE RESEARCH WITH RECREATION.)*

**Anthony:** Let's talk about psychedelic drugs.

There is serious medical research involving substances such as psilocybin and MDMA, particularly concerning certain psychiatric conditions.

**Dr. Chen:** Yes. But research protocols, medical supervision, and regulatory approval matter enormously.

**Anthony:** So the difference between medical research and someone taking a mystery substance at a music festival is documentation?

**Dr. Chen:** Documentation, screening, controlled conditions, monitoring, and follow-up.

**Agent Smith:** We are monitoring all unusual psychological activity.

**Anthony:** Are you a psychiatrist?

**Agent Smith:** No.

**Anthony:** A neurologist?

**Agent Smith:** No.

**Anthony:** Then why are you monitoring psychological activity?

**Agent Smith:** I have a government badge.

**Anthony:** That's not a medical degree! That's a Halloween costume with a pension!

*(The doctors laugh.)*

**Nurse Donut:** We have a new experimental treatment.

**Anthony:** What is it?

**Nurse Donut:** Chocolate therapy.

**Dr. Chen:** That's not an approved treatment for psychiatric illness.

**Anthony:** It is for the hospital administration. Give them a dozen and maybe they'll stop sending invoices.

## **Scene 5: The Medical Images That Nobody Wants to Look At**

*(A radiology department. An enormous monitor displays an MRI scan.)*

**Anthony:** Now we get to the interesting part. Medical imaging.

You've got MRI, CT scans, X-rays, ultrasound, pathology images. These are supposed to help doctors understand what's happening inside a human body.

**Dr. Nguyen:** Correct. Imaging helps physicians identify abnormalities, assess injuries, and monitor disease.

**Anthony:** Then why does this hospital have an intelligence department that seems more interested in the images than the doctors?

**Agent Smith:** We use advanced visual analysis.

**Anthony:** For medicine?

**Agent Smith:** For situational awareness.

**Anthony:** Situational awareness? It's a knee X-ray!

**Dr. Chen:** Medical images should be interpreted in their clinical context by qualified professionals. Patient privacy and proper authorization are essential.

**Anthony:** So let me understand. A doctor needs a radiologist to examine a medical image, but an intelligence officer can look at it and suddenly become a strategic analyst?

**Agent Smith:** We have proprietary image-recognition capabilities.

**Anthony:** Wonderful. Can your proprietary technology tell whether I have a broken bone?

**Agent Smith:** No.

**Anthony:** Can it tell whether I have a tumor?

**Agent Smith:** No.

**Anthony:** Can it tell whether the donut is chocolate or vanilla?

**Agent Smith:** Yes.

**Anthony:** FINALLY! A breakthrough in American intelligence!

## **Scene 6: Why the World Health Organization Exists**

*(The WHO Administrator enters carrying a giant binder.)*

**WHO Administrator:** I understand there are concerns about international medical standards.

**Anthony:** Absolutely. We're trying to establish whether medicine is medicine, intelligence is intelligence, and a donut is still a donut.

**WHO Administrator:** The World Health Organization works with countries on public health, disease prevention, international health regulations, medical guidance, and cooperation.

**Anthony:** Including Vietnam and China?

**WHO Administrator:** Yes. WHO works with countries across the world, including the United States, China, and Vietnam.

**Anthony:** So what's the policy on childhood medical records?

**WHO Administrator:** Medical records should be maintained appropriately, patient confidentiality respected, and healthcare practices grounded in evidence.

**Anthony:** And medical imaging?

**WHO Administrator:** It should be used for legitimate clinical purposes with appropriate safeguards.

**Anthony:** And psychedelic research?

**WHO Administrator:** It requires scientific rigor, ethical oversight, and appropriate regulatory protections.

**Anthony:** And intelligence agencies?

**WHO Administrator:** They are not substitutes for healthcare professionals.

*(Long silence.)*

**Anthony:** Somebody finally said it!

**Agent Smith:** I object.

**WHO Administrator:** Your objection has been recorded.

**Anthony:** In a medical file?

**WHO Administrator:** No. In a donut box.

## **Scene 7: The Final International Diagnosis**

*(Everyone gathers around a conference table. Flags of the United States, China, and Vietnam are displayed.)*

**Anthony:** Here's what I think.

A country can have advanced technology, sophisticated intelligence agencies, extraordinary pharmaceutical manufacturing, and world-class hospitals.

But if a patient cannot understand their medical history, obtain their records, receive a proper explanation, or have their medical images evaluated appropriately, what exactly have we accomplished?

**Dr. Chen:** That's a legitimate question about healthcare transparency.

**Dr. Nguyen:** International medical cooperation should improve patient care, not undermine confidence.

**WHO Administrator:** Public trust depends on evidence, accountability, and reliable institutions.

**Agent Smith:** What about tactical intelligence?

**Anthony:** Tactical intelligence? You're in a hospital!

Your mission is to identify the enemy.

**Agent Smith:** Correct.

**Anthony:** The enemy is the $900 administrative fee!

*(Nurse Donut drops a giant invoice onto the table.)*

**Nurse Donut:** We've received the final bill.

**Anthony:** What's the diagnosis?

**Nurse Donut:** International medical confusion.

**Anthony:** Treatment?

**Nurse Donut:** More transparency, better records, qualified doctors, and fewer intelligence officers in the radiology department.

**Agent Smith:** I cannot approve that treatment.

**Anthony:** Then you're going to need a second opinion.

*(Anthony picks up a chocolate donut.)*

**Anthony:** And somebody please tell me whether this thing has been through clinical trials.

**Nurse Donut:** Three generations of clinical trials.

**Anthony:** On whom?

**Nurse Donut:** The hospital administration.

*(Everyone looks at Agent Smith.)*

**Agent Smith:** I have to leave.

**Anthony:** THERE'S YOUR FIRST POSITIVE MEDICAL OUTCOME!

*Curtain.*

### **Final stand-up closing: "The Global Healthcare Plan"**

"America has the MRI. China has the manufacturing. Vietnam has the determination. The World Health Organization has the paperwork. And the intelligence agencies have the surveillance.

But somehow, nobody has the original medical records from when I was five!

I've got one final recommendation for international healthcare cooperation: Let the doctors practice medicine, let the scientists conduct research, let the intelligence agencies investigate actual intelligence, and let the patients eat their donuts in peace.

Because if the entire world can coordinate a satellite launch, surely we can coordinate a medical appointment!"

**END.**

*Satirical note: The childhood serum, psychedelic research, and alleged intelligence involvement are treated as questions and comic premises, not established events. Establishing what treatment was given at age five would require actual medical records; medical imaging should be assessed through authorized clinical channels.*


# **HOT SLINGERS: THE NYU CAFETERIA CARTEL**

*A gangster comedy routine about cafeteria cooks, suspiciously powerful food, wild students, and chefs who run the streets with a spatula.*

**Setting:** NYU cafeteria, 12:15 PM. The lunch line is backed up. The cooks are running the operation. The students are about to discover that today's special comes with an attitude.

## **The opening: Who's running this kitchen?**

*(Anthony walks into the cafeteria wearing sunglasses, a leather jacket, and carrying a plastic lunch tray.)*

**ANTHONY:**

Yo, yo, yo! What's happening in this joint?

I heard the cooks are running a food operation.

These ain't ordinary cooks. These are hot slingers!

They got aprons, steel spatulas, and enough attitude to take over Manhattan.

I asked the head chef, "What's today's special?"

He says, "Star food."

I said, "What's star food?"

He says, "The kind that makes you feel like somebody owes you money."

I said, "You mean spicy?"

He says, "Nah, kid. I'm talking about attitude with a side of fries."

## **Ten gangster food jokes**

**1. The Wild Chicken**

Yo, the chef served some chicken at lunchtime.

Five minutes later, the students were running around the cafeteria like they just heard the rent went up.

One guy started yelling, "WHO TOOK MY FORK?"

Another one was arguing with the vending machine.

I asked the cook, "What'd you put in that chicken?"

He says, "A little seasoning."

I said, "Brother, that ain't seasoning. That's a personality transplant!"

**2. The Hot Slingers**

These cooks are hot slingers.

They don't serve food. They distribute consequences.

The chef throws a meatball onto your plate and says, "You want gravy with that, or you want to keep your dignity?"

I said, "Both."

He says, "We don't do both in this neighborhood."

**3. The Angry Spaghetti**

I saw a student eating spaghetti.

He took one bite, stood up, and started yelling at the entire table.

"YOU KNOW WHAT YOUR PROBLEM IS?"

Nobody had said anything to him!

The cook came over and said, "That's the angry marinara."

I said, "What's in it?"

He says, "Tomatoes, garlic, and a little family history."

**4. The Star Food VIP Treatment**

Apparently, some students get the special food.

They walk into the cafeteria like celebrities.

Everybody else gets a sandwich.

These guys get a golden tray, extra cheese, and a personal introduction from the chef.

I asked, "What's so special about them?"

The cook says, "They're VIP."

I said, "Very Important Palates?"

He says, "No, Very Irritated People."

**5. The Flying French Fries**

Yo! I saw a guy eat the hot fries.

He jumped up, started running around the cafeteria, and threw his backpack over his shoulder.

I yelled, "WHERE YOU GOING?"

He says, "I GOT PLACES TO BE!"

I said, "You just sat down!"

He says, "THAT WAS BEFORE THE POTATOES!"

The cook looks at me and says, "Extra crispy."

I said, "Extra what? Extra athletic?"

**6. The Gangster Burger**

The chef made a burger so big it needed its own attorney.

The student picked it up and said, "This burger looks like it has a criminal record."

He takes a bite.

Suddenly he's talking like a gangster.

"Listen, pal. Nobody touches my onion rings."

I said, "What's wrong with you?"

He says, "I'm having a beef with everybody."

I said, "That's because you're eating beef!"

**7. The Cafeteria Turf War**

The cooks have territory.

The pizza station controls the east side.

The burger station controls the west side.

The salad bar is neutral territory.

Nobody goes near the soup station after dark.

I asked why.

The chef says, "There's been trouble with the minestrone family."

I said, "What happened?"

He says, "Somebody crossed the broth."

**8. The Explosive Chili**

The cafeteria served chili.

One student ate a bowl and started giving a speech about international relations.

"WE NEED STRONGER BORDERS!"

I said, "You're eating beans, buddy."

He says, "THEN CLOSE THE BORDER!"

The chef looks over and says, "That's my three-alarm chili."

I said, "Three alarms? The fire department is already outside!"

**9. The Mysterious Dessert**

The chef brings out chocolate cake.

Everybody gets quiet.

One student takes a bite and starts laughing.

Another starts dancing.

A third one begins complimenting the cafeteria furniture.

I said, "What's in this cake?"

The chef says, "Chocolate, flour, sugar, and happiness."

I said, "Then why is that guy negotiating with a chair?"

He says, "He's having a good dessert experience."

**10. The Final Cafeteria Boss**

The head chef walks into the dining room.

Everybody stops talking.

He puts down his spatula.

"LISTEN UP! WHO'S BEEN COMPLAINING ABOUT THE FOOD?"

Nobody moves.

He says, "I'M THE BOSS OF THIS KITCHEN!"

One student raises his hand.

"Can I have another serving?"

The chef looks at him.

"YOU GOT SOME NERVE, KID!"

The student says, "Yeah. I ate the spicy chicken."

The chef smiles.

"That's my boy. You got the hot slinger special!"

## **Final scene: The NYU Food Mafia**

*(Anthony approaches the head chef.)*

**ANTHONY:** Listen, chef. I figured out your whole operation.

**CHEF:** What operation?

**ANTHONY:** You feed the students, they get wild, they start talking tough, they run around, and suddenly everybody thinks they're the boss.

**CHEF:** That's called student life.

**ANTHONY:** And what's the secret ingredient?

**CHEF:** Love.

**ANTHONY:** Love?

**CHEF:** Yeah. And 40 pounds of hot sauce.

**ANTHONY:** You know what, chef? You're not running a cafeteria.

You're running a culinary crime family.

**CHEF:** Watch your mouth, kid.

**ANTHONY:** Why?

**CHEF:** Because you're standing in my kitchen.

**ANTHONY:** And?

**CHEF:** And dessert is served by appointment only.

*(Anthony slowly puts his sunglasses back on.)*

**ANTHONY:** I knew it. The cooks rule the world.

**CHEF:** Now get out of my line.

**ANTHONY:** You got it, boss. One thing, though...

**CHEF:** What?

**ANTHONY:** Can I get another meatball?

**CHEF:** YOU GOT A LOT OF NERVE!

**ANTHONY:** That's what happens when you eat at NYU!

*Lights out. Gangster jazz plays.*

# **NATO: THE ALLIANCE OF FREEDOM, HOSPITALS & SUBWOOFERS**

*A dark political comedy about American leadership, regulated freedom, international healthcare, digestive disasters, and the world's most dangerous bathroom stereo system.*

**Style:** Gangster satire meets bad 1980s horror movie, with geopolitical absurdity and medical slapstick.

## **Scene 1: America, the leader of the free world**

*(A dramatic military soundtrack. A NATO flag waves behind a hospital reception desk.)*

**ANTHONY:**

Ladies and gentlemen, welcome to NATO!

The North Atlantic Treaty Organization!

America's great international alliance!

We've got 32 countries, military cooperation, collective defense, and apparently one enormous health insurance policy that nobody knows how to use.

America is the leader.

We're exporting freedom!

Regulated freedom!

Personally approved freedom!

You can be free to do whatever you want, provided you fill out the appropriate paperwork, get three signatures, and don't forget your hospital deductible.

That's American democracy!

Article 5 says an attack against one member is an attack against all.

Article 6 says your hospital bill is an attack against your checking account.

And Article 7 says if you eat something questionable in the cafeteria, you're on your own!

*(Pause.)*

I went to the hospital and asked if America was part of the healthcare alliance.

They said, "Sir, we're not part of NATO."

I said, "Then why does this hospital look like a military installation?"

They said, "Because we're defending your wallet."

## **Scene 2: The horror movie called Healthcare**

*(Thunder. A hospital corridor. A nurse slowly pushes a wheelchair through the darkness.)*

You ever notice American healthcare looks like a bad horror movie?

The lights flicker.

The elevator doesn't work.

There's a mysterious noise coming from the digestive department.

And somewhere in the distance, a doctor whispers:

"Your insurance doesn't cover that."

That's when the music starts!

DUN! DUN! DUUUUN!

The patient is running down the hallway.

The hospital administrator is chasing him with a clipboard.

The billing department is behind them with a calculator.

And the surgeon?

He's just standing there saying:

"Does anybody know where the cafeteria is?"

America has the world's most advanced technology.

We can send satellites into orbit.

We can photograph the surface of Mars.

We can identify a submarine from 500 miles away.

But somehow, when I ask for my medical records, they need a search warrant and a flashlight!

## **Scene 3: The world's shortage of love**

Here's the really frightening part.

Imagine a world where people don't have enough love.

Not enough compassion.

Not enough patience.

Not enough respect for one another.

Everybody's measuring their success in money.

Some people have so much money they don't know what to do with it.

Other people are struggling to buy groceries.

And somewhere in the middle, somebody decides that violence is a business model.

That's not economic development!

That's a horror movie written by an accountant!

You've got people selling fear, buying weapons, and calculating the financial return on human suffering.

What happened to the old-fashioned economy?

You know, where somebody made a sandwich and everybody went home happy?

Now the international financial system needs a therapist.

**America says:** We're going to lead the free world!

**The world says:** Great! Can we have some healthcare?

**America says:** We have a defense budget!

**The world says:** That's nice. Can we have some affordable medicine?

**America says:** We have an aircraft carrier!

**The world says:** Does it have a pharmacy?

*(Pause.)*

That's the problem with international leadership.

Everybody wants to command the fleet.

Nobody wants to clean the kitchen.

## **Scene 4: The digestive defense department**

*(Anthony enters wearing a white laboratory coat and holding a blender full of slushies.)*

Now let's discuss the digestive system.

America has a Department of Defense.

I think we need a Department of Digestion.

Because some of these hospitals are moving slower than a government procurement contract.

The intestines are backed up.

The stomach is protesting.

The colon has declared independence.

And the patient is sitting there asking:

"Doctor, what's the treatment?"

The doctor says, "More fluids."

I said, "What kind?"

He says, "Water."

I said, "What about a slushie?"

He says, "That's not a medical strategy."

I said, "You haven't met my digestive system."

Sometimes you need a little ice, a little hydration, and a vacation from the cafeteria.

Of course, if you're dealing with persistent digestive problems, you need an actual medical assessment, not a military intervention.

But the way these hospitals operate, I'm expecting NATO to send a peacekeeping mission into my small intestine.

**General:** We have established a humanitarian corridor.

**Doctor:** That's the colon.

**General:** We are proceeding with Operation Smooth Passage.

**Patient:** Somebody please call the plumber!

## **Scene 5: The Philips bathroom sound system**

*(A spotlight hits an enormous subwoofer next to a toilet.)*

Now, let's talk about laxatives.

I always thought the best one was named after a stereo equipment company.

Philips!

I figured if anybody could move something, it would be Philips.

That thing didn't just have a bass note.

It had a bowel movement with Dolby Surround Sound!

You take one dose and suddenly your bathroom has a sound system.

BOOOOOOM!

The neighbors think you've installed a ported subwoofer.

The guy downstairs calls building management.

"There's a strange harmonic frequency coming through the plumbing!"

I said, "That's not a stereo installation. That's a medical emergency with a crossover network!"

You've got tuned harmonics.

You've got a ported enclosure.

You've got a 12-inch driver.

And you've got one very nervous man asking whether the volume can be turned down.

The doctor says, "How are you feeling?"

I say, "A little lighter."

He says, "Any side effects?"

I say, "My bathroom has become a concert venue."

**Doctor:** What frequency?

**Anthony:** Around 40 hertz.

**Doctor:** That's low.

**Anthony:** Not as low as my dignity!

## **Scene 6: NATO's international medical summit**

*(A conference table. Flags of America, Britain, France, Germany, and other NATO countries.)*

**AMERICAN GENERAL:** We need international cooperation.

**BRITISH DELEGATE:** On defense?

**AMERICAN GENERAL:** On defense, energy, food, and healthcare.

**FRENCH DELEGATE:** What about quality of life?

**AMERICAN GENERAL:** Absolutely.

**GERMAN DELEGATE:** What about medical infrastructure?

**AMERICAN GENERAL:** We'll establish a committee.

**ANTHONY:** What about the slushies?

*(Silence.)*

**AMERICAN GENERAL:** Excuse me?

**ANTHONY:** The slushies! The digestive infrastructure! The Philips subwoofer! We've got people suffering in the bathroom while you're discussing military logistics!

**FRENCH DELEGATE:** We have excellent pastries.

**ANTHONY:** That's not helping!

**BRITISH DELEGATE:** Perhaps a cup of tea?

**ANTHONY:** Now we're getting somewhere.

**AMERICAN GENERAL:** We need a strategic plan.

**ANTHONY:** Here's the plan:

- Feed people.
- Treat people.
- Protect people.
- Stop turning everything into a financial transaction.
- And install better plumbing.

**GERMAN DELEGATE:** That's surprisingly practical.

**ANTHONY:** That's because I have a working digestive system and a basic understanding of civilization!

## **Scene 7: The final horror**

*(Lights go dark. A hospital monitor beeps. A NATO general appears in silhouette.)*

**GENERAL:** America stands ready to defend freedom.

**ANTHONY:** Wonderful.

**GENERAL:** We will protect international stability.

**ANTHONY:** Excellent.

**GENERAL:** We will strengthen cooperation.

**ANTHONY:** Fantastic.

**GENERAL:** We will establish a new international regulatory framework.

**ANTHONY:** Oh, no.

**GENERAL:** Every citizen will be required to register their digestive system.

**ANTHONY:** WHAT?!

**GENERAL:** All bowel movements will be subject to international observation.

**ANTHONY:** This is no longer NATO. This is the North Atlantic Toilet Organization!

**GENERAL:** Your slushie consumption requires authorization.

**ANTHONY:** I'M LEAVING!

**GENERAL:** You cannot leave. Your bathroom is a strategic asset.

*(A deep bass note sounds.)*

BOOOOOOOOOOM.

**GENERAL:** What was that?

**ANTHONY:** Philips.

*(The general slowly turns toward the bathroom.)*

**GENERAL:** Evacuate the building.

**ANTHONY:** Finally! An international decision I agree with!

*Lights out.*
