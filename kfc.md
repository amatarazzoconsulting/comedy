**The concept:** What if KFC stopped being an ordinary fast-food restaurant and became a fully functioning chicken kingdom? The employees don't simply take orders. They cluck them. The cashiers speak a strange combination of English and chicken language. Employees walk like chickens, flap their wings, peck at imaginary objects, and treat every customer as an honored visitor to the coop.

Anthony arrives with a revolutionary business proposal: if the restaurant is named Kentucky Fried Chicken, why are the employees behaving like ordinary people?

His answer is simple: **Everybody cluck. Everybody walk. Everybody serve.**

This is a fictional comedy production, not an official KFC program.

# **ACT III — THE CHICKEN LANGUAGE ACADEMY**

The cashier must be able to speak enough ordinary English to make the order clear, but every sentence should contain a little chicken personality.

The key is to make the clucking expressive rather than incomprehensible.

## **The chicken vocabulary**

| Ordinary English     | Chicken translation            |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Hello!               | Cluck-cluck! Hello!            |
| Welcome!             | Welcome to the coop! Cluck!    |
| What would you like? | What'll it be, cluck-cluck?    |
| One moment           | Peck a moment!                 |
| Certainly            | Clucksolutely!                 |
| Yes                  | Bawk!                          |
| No                   | Cluck no!                      |
| Thank you            | Cluck you kindly!              |
| Your order is ready  | Your chicken awaits!           |
| Have a nice day      | Have a clucking wonderful day! |
| Goodbye              | Bawk-bye!                      |
| Please wait          | Hold your feathers!            |

## **The pronunciation guide**

The cashier should use three main sounds:

- **Cluck:** A friendly, conversational sound.
- **Bawk:** A loud, confident announcement.
- **Brrr-cluck:** An excited or confused chicken reaction.

The cashier can combine them with regular English.

Examples:

- "Cluck-cluck, how may I help you?"
- "Bawk! That's a mighty fine order!"
- "One moment, please. My feathers are checking."
- "Clucksolutely! We can do that!"
- "Your total is $14.75. Bawk!"

The important rule: customers must always understand the actual order, price, and instructions.

## **The five official chicken walks**

**1. The Standard Chicken Walk**

- Small quick steps.
- Head moves gently forward and back.
- Elbows held slightly away from the body like wings.
- Keep the movement playful, not frantic.

**2. The Proud Rooster Strut**

- Chest forward.
- Chin up.
- Slow, confident steps.
- Occasionally announce: "Cock-a-doodle-doo!"

**3. The Curious Chicken Peck**

- Take two steps.
- Tilt head.
- Look down at an imaginary object.
- Make one soft "cluck."
- Continue walking.

**4. The Excited Nugget Shuffle**

- Quick little steps in place.
- Two small wing flaps.
- A loud "Bawk-bawk-bawk!"
- Freeze dramatically.

**5. The Chicken Emergency Walk**

- Fast but controlled steps.
- Arms tucked in.
- Head looking toward the destination.
- Announce: "Cluck! Order up!"

All movements should be performed in designated safe areas, with clear vision and no running, slippery footwear, or movements that interfere with food handling.
