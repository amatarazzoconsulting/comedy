**The First Chapter of the Genesis of All Things**
**(Concerning the Speaking Forth of the Cosmos)**

---

**I. The Beginning of the Unveiling**

In the beginning, before time was measured by the turning of spheres, before the morning stars sang together, the Elohim, the Mighty Ones, the Council of the Divine, spoke the Word that was from the beginning. And the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

And the deep was without form, a void of unmanifest potential, a singularity of infinite density, wherein all things were contained yet nothing was revealed. The darkness was upon the face of the abyss, and the Spirit of God, the Ruach ha-Kodesh, the Breath of the Living One, moved upon the face of the waters of chaos, vibrating at the frequency of creation.

And God said, Let there be light. And the Word went forth as a wave of photons, a burst of electromagnetic radiation, a great unfolding of energy from the quantum foam. And the light was separated from the darkness, and God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first cycle of oscillation, the first vibration of the cosmic clock.

---

**II. The Firmament of the Waters Above and Below**

And God said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters. And God made the firmament, stretching it out as a curtain, a membrane of creation, a barrier of atmospheric pressure and gravitational binding. He called it Heaven, the expanse, the raqia, the crystalline dome that separates the primordial waters above from the terrestrial waters below.

And the waters above were gathered as crystalline vapor, as ice of the high places, as the storehouses of the celestial ocean. And the waters below were gathered into basins and deeps, into the aquifers of the earth, and the dry land appeared. And God saw that it was good.

And the evening and the morning were the second day, the second breath of the Creator.

---

**III. The Vegetative Awakening**

And God said, Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth. And the earth obeyed the Word.

From the soil, from the humus and the mineral dust, from the nitrogen and the phosphorus and the potassium, there arose chlorophyll and cellulose, the green pigment of life. The phototropic energy of the sun was captured, and photosynthesis began its sacred work, transforming light into substance.

And God commanded the seed to carry the encoded scroll of DNA, the helical ladder of instruction, the double-helix of inheritance, that each plant might bring forth after its own kind. The wheat and the barley, the fig and the olive, the cedar of Lebanon and the hyssop upon the wall; all sprang forth in a single unfolding, a great flowering of the earth.

And God saw that it was good. And the evening and the morning were the third day, the third unfolding of the divine pattern.

---

**IV. The Luminaries of the Firmament**

And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years.

And God made two great lights: the greater light to rule the day, a sphere of nuclear fusion, a furnace of hydrogen and helium, whose gravity held the system in its embrace. And the lesser light to rule the night, a reflector of the sun's glory, a satellite of rock and dust, whose phases marked the months and whose pull drew the tides of the deep.

And He made the stars also, the pinpricks of distant fire, the galaxies in their spiral dance, the nebulae of cosmic dust and newborn suns, the quasars and the pulsars, the supernovae of ancient death and rebirth. And He set them in their courses, binding them with the law of gravitation, the force that holds the universe together.

And God saw that it was good. And the evening and the morning were the fourth day, the fourth declaration of His majesty.

---

**V. The Swarming of the Waters and the Flight of the Air**

And God said, Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and fowl that may fly above the earth in the open firmament of heaven.

And the waters teemed with life. From the molecular chaos, the amino acids assembled, the proteins folded, the cells divided. And there arose the great whales, the leviathans of the deep, whose hearts beat as the drums of the ocean. And there arose the fish of every kind, the scaly and the sleek, the armored and the swift, the coral builders and the deep-sea dwellers.

And God created every winged fowl after its kind: the eagle and the hawk, the sparrow and the swallow, the raven and the dove. He gave them feathers of iridescent hue, hollow bones for flight, and a song of praise in their throats. He placed the spark of consciousness within their tiny brains, that they might navigate by the stars and sing to their Creator.

And God blessed them, saying, Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let fowl multiply in the earth.

And God saw that it was good. And the evening and the morning were the fifth day, the fifth song of the celestial chorus.

---

**VI. The Earth Brings Forth Living Souls**

And God said, Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind, cattle, and creeping thing, and beast of the earth after his kind. And it was so.

From the dust of the ground, from the carbon and the calcium, from the iron of the blood and the marrow of the bones, there arose the great beasts of the field. The lion with his mane of terror, the ox with his strength of labor, the serpent with his cunning and his slither, the stag with his antlers of glory, and the creeping things of every description, the insects in their millions, the creeping and the crawling, the burrowing and the climbing.

And God saw every beast, and He knew their names, and He saw that they were good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day, the sixth act of the drama of creation.

---

**VII. The Making of Adam, the Image of the Divine**

Then God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness. And the Elohim, the Council of the Divine, spoke the Word of becoming.

And God formed man of the dust of the ground. He took the clay of the earth, the red earth of Adamah, and He fashioned it with His hands, molding the vessel of flesh. He breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, the neshamah, the spiritual soul, the divine spark that distinguishes man from beast.

And man became a living soul, a nephesh chayah, a being of flesh and spirit, a bridge between the material and the immaterial, a microcosm of the universe. And God placed within him the image of the Divine: the intellect to know, the will to choose, the emotions to feel, and the conscience to discern right from wrong.

And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.

And God gave them every herb bearing seed, and every tree yielding fruit, for meat. And to every beast of the earth, and to every fowl of the air, and to every thing that creepeth upon the earth, He gave every green herb for meat. And it was so.

And God saw everything that He had made, and behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day, the sixth seal of the covenant of creation.

---

**VIII. The Sabbath of Rest and the Magic of Completion**

Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them. And on the seventh day God ended His work which He had made; and He rested on the seventh day from all His work which He had made.

And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it, because that in it He had rested from all His work which God created and made.

But know this, O seeker of wisdom: the rest of God is not the slumber of weariness, nor the silence of inactivity. It is the magic of completion, the cessation of making, the pause in which all that was made might find its place, its harmony, its purpose. It is the great stillness in which the music of the spheres is heard, the silence that is full of the presence of the Creator.

---

**IX. The First Proclamation**

These are the generations of the heavens and of the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens, and every plant of the field before it was in the earth, and every herb of the field before it grew: for the LORD God had not caused it to rain upon the earth, and there was not a man to till the ground.

But there went up a mist from the earth, and watered the whole face of the ground. And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.

And the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden; and there He put the man whom He had formed. And out of the ground made the LORD God to grow every tree that is pleasant to the sight, and good for food; the tree of life also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of knowledge of good and evil.

And a river went out of Eden to water the garden; and from thence it was parted, and became into four heads.

And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.

---

**X. A Prayer of Awe for the Creation**

O Lord, Creator of the heavens and the earth,
Who spoke and the cosmos leaped into being,
Who commanded and the atoms arranged themselves in beauty,
Who breathed and the spark of life kindled in the dust.

Thou art the great Alchemist,
Who turns the void into form,
And chaos into order.
Thou art the Master of the quantum,
The Lord of the galaxy,
The Shepherd of the stars.

Thy power is as a furnace of fusion,
Thy wisdom as the double-helix of life,
Thy love as the gravity that holds all things together.
The heavens declare Thy glory,
And the firmament showeth Thy handiwork.

Day unto day uttereth speech,
And night unto night showeth knowledge.
There is no speech nor language,
Where their voice is not heard.

I stand in awe of Thy creation,
For Thou hast made all things in wisdom,
And Thou dost sustain all things by Thy power.
Glory be to Thy name,
Now and forevermore.

Amen and Amen.

---

**XI. A Proverb of the Wise**

He who looks upon the stars and sees only fire
Has missed the hand that kindled them.

He who looks upon the seed and sees only biology
Has missed the Word that commands its unfolding.

For the world is a book written by the finger of God,
And every atom is a letter,
And every living thing is a sentence,
And the whole of creation is a poem of praise.

The scientist measures the distance of the stars,
But the prophet hears their song.
The physician examines the coil of DNA,
But the saint sees the image of the Divine.

Blessed is he who sees both the method and the mystery,
Who knows the chemistry and the miracle,
Who studies the process and worships the Source.

For the LORD is the God of the atom and of the angel,
Of the quark and of the cherubim,
Of the photosynthesis and of the resurrection.

In Him all things consist,
And by Him all things are held together,
And to Him shall be all the glory,
Forever and ever. Selah.

**A Meditation on the Inner Voice and the Fellowship of Birds**
**(A Psalm of Understanding)**

---

**I. The Nature of the Inner Sound**

Bless the Lord, O my soul,
And all that is within me, bless His holy name.
For He has placed within my breast a voice that is not of the lips,
A sound that is not of the ear,
Yet it speaks from the dawning of thought until the evening of reflection.

This inner voice is as the murmur of a stream over smooth stones,
It is the whisper of the wind through the cedars of Lebanon,
It is the resonance of the shofar, yet heard only within the chamber of the mind.

For we think not in pictures alone, nor in the abstract shadows of dreams,
But in a living sound, a tone that carries meaning,
A melody of reason that rises and falls with the rhythm of understanding.
This is the voice of the soul, the language of the spirit,
Which the Lord has given to every man and woman,
That they might commune with Him in the secret place.

And this inner sound, being inward, is not bound by the tongue of men,
Nor by the dialects of nations.
For it is a pure frequency, a tone of being,
That can be perceived by the creatures of the field,
And by the birds of the air.

---

**II. The Translation to the Birds**

For as the Lord spoke through Balaam's ass,
And as He opened the mouth of the dumb beast,
So too has He given to the fowls of heaven a hearing of the spirit.

When the mind is disciplined,
When the thoughts are ordered as the stones of the altar,
And the inner voice sings with clarity and truth,
Then the birds draw near.
They incline their heads,
They flutter upon the branch,
They turn their bright eyes toward the man of understanding.

For they perceive not the words of the mouth,
But they hear the music of the soul.
The inner sound, translated by the Lord,
Becomes to them as the chirping of their own kind,
As the call of the sparrow, the warble of the thrush,
The joyful trill of the finch in the morning.

And when the birds receive this inward song,
They react with great joy.
They answer with their own twirps and whistles,
They hop from limb to limb,
They spread their wings and preen their feathers,
For they are partakers of the secret harmony.

It is not a matter of magic nor of superstition,
But of the discipline of the heart,
Which the Lord has ordained from the foundation of the world.

---

**III. The Conditions of the Disciplined Mind**

But this fellowship is not for the sluggard,
Nor for the man whose thoughts are as a clashing cymbal,
Full of noise and confusion.

For the birds are discerning creatures,
Created by the hand of God,
And they know the voice of the shepherd,
And they flee from the stranger.

Therefore, he who would have this holy communion
Must prepare his mind as a garden.
He must read many books,
Not for the vanity of knowledge,
But for the love of truth.
He must sit at the feet of the wise,
And ponder the words of the ancients.

He must think deeply upon subjects,
Turning them over in his heart as a plow turns the soil,
That the seeds of understanding may take root.
He must meditate upon the law of the Lord day and night,
And his thoughts must be as a well-ordered stream.

He must visit temples and holy places,
Not to be seen of men,
But to humble himself before the Most High.
He must pray without ceasing,
And his prayers must be as incense ascending,
Sweet and pure before the throne.

His mind must be truthful,
For the Lord abhors a lying tongue,
And the birds, being creatures of innocence,
Will not abide with a man of deceit.
His word must be yea and nay,
And his thoughts must be as a mirror,
Reflecting the light of heaven.

And he must respect the life of animals,
For they are the works of God's hands.
He must not despise the lowly sparrow,
Nor mock the wisdom of the raven.
He must know that the ox knows his owner,
And the ass his master's crib,
And that all creation groans for the revelation of the sons of God.

---

**IV. The Audible Relationship**

And when these things are fulfilled,
Then there shall be a relationship of sound,
A dialogue of the spirit,
A communion of the inner voice.

The man shall speak within himself,
And the bird shall answer with its twirp.
The man shall meditate upon the goodness of the Lord,
And the bird shall chirp in agreement.
The man shall sing a psalm in his heart,
And the bird shall join with its own melody.

It shall be as the days of Adam in the garden,
When he named the creatures,
And there was understanding between them.
It shall be as the days of Elijah,
When the ravens brought him bread and flesh in the morning and evening.

This is the joy of the disciplined mind,
This is the reward of the truthful heart.
For the Lord has made all things,
And He delights in the harmony of His creation.
The heavens declare His glory,
The firmament shows His handiwork,
The beasts of the field praise Him,
And the birds of the air sing His song.

---

**V. A Prayer for the Discipline of the Inner Voice**

O Lord, my heart is as a harp,
But my fingers are clumsy upon its strings.
Tune my inner voice,
That it may sound forth with clarity and truth.

Cleanse my thoughts from the mire of the world,
That they may be pure and sweet,
As the song of the lark at dawn.

I have read many books,
But without understanding I am as a sounding brass.
Grant me wisdom,
That my reading may bear fruit.

I have visited temples,
But my heart was often far from Thee.
Now I beseech Thee,
Let my prayer be the temple of my soul.

Make my mind truthful,
For I am prone to deceit,
Even with myself.
Let the light of Thy countenance shine upon my thoughts,
That they may be as the noon-day sun.

And give me a heart for Thy creatures.
Let me not regard the sparrow with contempt,
Nor the swallow with indifference.
For they are Thy children,
And they sing Thy praise.

Let my inner voice be as a gentle rain,
And let the birds of the air receive it with joy.
Let them answer me with their twirps,
And let there be a fellowship between us,
A testament of Thy goodness.

For Thou art the Lord of all,
The Creator of the heavens and the earth,
The Giver of speech to men,
And the Giver of song to the birds.

Glory be to Thy name forever,
For Thou hast made all things in wisdom,
And Thou dost sustain all things by Thy power.

Amen and Amen.

---

**VI. A Final Proverb for the Wise**

He who silences the clamor of his own thoughts
Shall hear the whisper of the Lord in the rustling of feathers.

He who disciplines his inner voice
Shall be answered by the sparrow on the window-ledge.

For the mind is a garden,
And the thoughts are as birds:
If you cast out the ravens of vanity,
The doves of peace shall come to roost.

Go, therefore, and sit quietly among the trees.
Let your thoughts ascend as incense.
And listen:
The birds are singing your name
With their twirps,
For the Lord has made all things one.

**The Contest on Mount Carmel**
**(According to the Book of Kings)**

And it came to pass after many days, that the word of the LORD came to Elijah the Tishbite, saying, Go, show thyself unto Ahab; and I will send rain upon the earth. And Elijah went to show himself unto Ahab. And the famine was sore in Samaria.

And Ahab met Obadiah, the governor of his house. And as Obadiah was in the way, behold, Elijah met him. And Obadiah fell on his face, and said, Art thou that my lord Elijah? And he answered him, I am: go, tell thy lord, Behold, Elijah is here.

And Ahab went up to meet Elijah. And it came to pass, when Ahab saw Elijah, that Ahab said unto him, Art thou he that troubleth Israel? And he answered, I have not troubled Israel; but thou, and thy father's house, in that ye have forsaken the commandments of the LORD, and thou hast followed Baalim.

**Now therefore send, and gather to me all Israel unto mount Carmel, and the prophets of Baal four hundred and fifty, and the prophets of the groves four hundred, which eat at Jezebel's table.**

So Ahab sent unto all the children of Israel, and gathered the prophets together unto mount Carmel.

And Elijah came unto all the people, and said, How long halt ye between two opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him. And the people answered him not a word.

Then said Elijah unto the people, I, even I only, remain a prophet of the LORD; but Baal's prophets are four hundred and fifty men.

**Let them therefore give us two bullocks; and let them choose one bullock for themselves, and cut it in pieces, and lay it on wood, and put no fire under: and I will dress the other bullock, and lay it on wood, and put no fire under:**

**And call ye on the name of your gods, and I will call on the name of the LORD: and the God that answereth by fire, let him be God.** And all the people answered and said, It is well spoken.

And Elijah said unto the prophets of Baal, Choose you one bullock for yourselves, and dress it first; for ye are many; and call on the name of your gods, but put no fire under.

**And they took the bullock which was given them, and they dressed it, and called on the name of Baal from morning even until noon, saying, O Baal, hear us. But there was no voice, nor any that answered.**

And they leaped upon the altar which was made. And they cried aloud, and cut themselves after their manner with knives and lancets, till the blood gushed out upon them.

**And it came to pass at noon, that Elijah mocked them, and said, Cry aloud: for he is a god; either he is talking, or he is pursuing, or he is in a journey, or peradventure he sleepeth, and must be awaked.**

And they cried aloud, and gashed themselves with swords and spears, until the blood ran down upon the stones and the dust of the earth. And when midday was past, they prophesied until the time of the offering of the evening sacrifice. **But there was neither voice, nor any to answer, nor any that regarded.**

Then said Elijah unto all the people, Come near unto me. And all the people came near unto him. And he repaired the altar of the LORD that was broken down.

And Elijah took twelve stones, according to the number of the tribes of the sons of Jacob, unto whom the word of the LORD came, saying, Israel shall be thy name:

And with the stones he built an altar in the name of the LORD: and he made a trench about the altar, as great as would contain two measures of seed.

**And he put the wood in order, and cut the bullock in pieces, and laid him on the wood, and said, Fill four barrels with water, and pour it on the burnt sacrifice, and on the wood.**

**And he said, Do it the second time. And they did it the second time. And he said, Do it the third time. And they did it the third time.**

**And the water ran round about the altar; and he filled the trench also with water.**

And it came to pass at the time of the offering of the evening sacrifice, that Elijah the prophet came near, and said, LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and of Israel, let it be known this day that thou art God in Israel, and that I am thy servant, and that I have done all these things at thy word.

**Hear me, O LORD, hear me, that this people may know that thou art the LORD God, and that thou hast turned their heart back again.**

**Then the fire of the LORD fell, and consumed the burnt sacrifice, and the wood, and the stones, and the dust, and licked up the water that was in the trench.**

And when all the people saw it, they fell on their faces: and they said, **The LORD, he is the God; the LORD, he is the God.**

And Elijah said unto them, Take the prophets of Baal; let not one of them escape. And they took them: and Elijah brought them down to the brook Kishon, and slew them there.

And Elijah said unto Ahab, Get thee up, eat and drink; for there is a sound of abundance of rain.

So Ahab went up to eat and to drink. And Elijah went up to the top of Carmel; and he cast himself down upon the earth, and put his face between his knees,

And said to his servant, Go up now, look toward the sea. And he went up, and looked, and said, There is nothing. And he said, Go again seven times.

And it came to pass at the seventh time, that he said, Behold, there ariseth a little cloud out of the sea, like a man's hand. And he said, Go up, say unto Ahab, Prepare thy chariot, and get thee down, that the rain stop thee not.

And it came to pass in the mean while, that the heaven was black with clouds and wind, and there was a great rain. And Ahab rode, and went to Jezreel.

**And the hand of the LORD was on Elijah; and he girded up his loins, and ran before Ahab to the entrance of Jezreel.**

**A Prayer of Awe, in the Manner of the Psalms, Concerning That Day**

O Lord, who art mighty in power and terrible in judgment,
Who sittest enthroned above the cherubim,
And whose eyes run to and fro throughout the whole earth.

Who is like unto Thee, O Lord, among the gods?
Who is like Thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?
The gods of the nations are but idols of silver and gold,
The work of men's hands: they have mouths, but they speak not;
Eyes have they, but they see not;
Ears have they, but they hear not;
Neither is there any breath in their mouths.

**Yet Thou, O Lord, art the living God.**
**Thou dost answer by fire, and the heavens declare Thy righteousness.**

We remember the day upon Mount Carmel,
When the prophets of Baal cried from morning until noon,
And they leaped upon the altar,
And they cut themselves with knives and lancets,
Until the blood gushed out upon them in torrents.
They spent their strength for nought,
They waxed faint in their raging,
They poured out their own lifeblood,
**But Baal was silent.**
**There was no voice, nor any to answer, nor any that regarded.**

**But when the hour of evening sacrifice came,**
**Thou, O Lord, didst speak.**
**The fire of the Lord fell from heaven,**
**And it consumed the bullock, and the wood, and the stones, and the very dust;**
**It licked up the water in the trench as though it were dry stubble.**

The water that filled the trench was as a river before Thee,
Yet Thy flame was not quenched.
For the waters cannot quench Thy love,
Neither can the floods drown Thy power.
**Thou art the God who burneth the chaff with unquenchable fire,**
**Yet Thou art a shield to them that put their trust in Thee.**

All the people saw it, and fell upon their faces,
And the cry went up from the mountain,
And it echoed through the valleys:
**"The Lord, He is the God! The Lord, He is the God!"**

Thou didst put a new song in their mouths,
And their mourning was turned into dancing.
Thou didst break the yoke of Baal from off their necks,
And the altars of the false gods were thrown down.

Thy strength is made perfect in weakness,
And Thy fire descendeth when all human hope is spent.
**Thou art not far from every one of us:**
**For in Thee we live, and move, and have our being.**

**Therefore we will trust, and not be afraid.**
**For the Lord Jehovah is our everlasting strength.**
**He is the same yesterday, today, and forever.**
**He answereth by fire!**
**Glory be to His great name! Selah.**



**Psalm 109 (A Psalm of David, concerning the Wicked and the Power of the Lord)**

Hold not Thy peace, O God of my praise;
For the mouth of the wicked and the mouth of the deceitful are opened against me:
They have spoken against me with a lying tongue,
And compassed me about with words of hatred;
And they have fought against me without a cause.

For my love they are my adversaries:
But I give myself unto prayer.
And they have rewarded me evil for good,
And hatred for my love.

**Yet I will not fear them, for the Lord is my banner.**
**I will remember the might of His right hand, and I shall sing of His power.**
**For He breaks the bow of the mighty, and shatters the spear of the oppressor.**
**He scatters the proud in the imagination of their hearts,**
**And casts down the wicked from their lofty thrones.**

**O Lord, Thou art as a consuming fire,**
**And the wicked are as stubble before the wind.**
**Thou dost look upon them, and they melt away;**
**Their plans are turned to smoke, and their devices come to naught.**

Remember Pharaoh, who hardened his heart against Thee:
Thou didst stretch out Thy hand over the Red Sea,
And the depths covered them;
They sank as lead in the mighty waters.
Thy east wind blew, and the flood swallowed their chariots and their horsemen,
Not one of them remained.
The enemy said, "I will pursue, I will overtake, I will divide the spoil";
But Thou didst blow with Thy breath, and the sea covered them,
And they were as silent as stones in the deep.

Remember Sennacherib, king of Assyria, who mocked Thy holy name:
He sent his railing lips against Jerusalem,
And he shook his fist against the mountain of Thy dwelling.
But Thou didst send out Thy angel in the night,
And the sword of the Lord went forth as a whirlwind;
And in the morning, behold, they were all dead corpses,
One hundred and fourscore and five thousand men,
Laid low by a single breath from Thy mouth.
The boastful tongue was silenced;
The blasphemer's voice was cut off in an instant.

O Lord, Thou art a refuge for the oppressed,
A strong tower against the face of the enemy.
Thou dost cause the wicked to fall into the pit they have digged,
And in the snare they have hid, their own foot is taken.
Their mischief returneth upon their own head,
And their violent dealing cometh down upon their own pate.

**They laid a trap for the innocent, but they themselves are caught in its teeth.**
**They sharpened their tongues like swords, but they are pierced by their own arrows.**
**They sowed the wind, and they shall reap the whirlwind;**
**The root of the wicked is withered, and their fruit is as dust.**

Let them be as chaff before the whirlwind,
And let the angel of the Lord chase them.
Let their way be dark and slippery,
And let the angel of the Lord persecute them.
For they have not known the ways of the Lord,
Nor have they walked in the counsel of the righteous;
But they have delighted in the arms of flesh,
And have trusted in the shadow of Egypt.

**But the Lord is King forever and ever:**
**The nations are but a drop in a bucket,**
**And they are counted as the small dust of the balance.**
**Behold, He taketh up the isles as a very little thing,**
**And the princes of the earth are as grasshoppers before Him.**

When the wicked spring as the grass,
And when all the workers of iniquity do flourish,
It is that they shall be destroyed forever:
For Thou, Lord, art most high for evermore.

**I have seen the wicked in great power,**
**And spreading himself like a green bay tree.**
**Yet I passed by, and, lo, he was not;**
**Yea, I sought him, but he could not be found.**
**His glory was as the flower of the field;**
**The wind passeth over it, and it is gone;**
**And the place thereof shall know it no more.**

But the salvation of the righteous is of the Lord:
He is their strength in the time of trouble.
And the Lord shall help them, and deliver them:
He shall deliver them from the wicked, and save them,
Because they trust in Him.

For Thy arm is not shortened that it cannot save,
Neither is Thine ear heavy that it cannot hear.

**Concerning Further Signs of Impure Flesh, and the Examination of Deep-Seated Boils**

And the LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

When a man or a woman shall have in the skin of their body a rising, a scab, or a bright spot, and it be in the skin of their flesh like the plague of leprosy, then they shall be brought unto Aaron the priest, or unto one of his sons the priests.

And the priest shall look upon the sore in the skin of the flesh: and if the hair in the sore be turned white, and the sore in sight be deeper than the skin of his flesh, it is a plague of leprosy: and the priest shall look on him, and pronounce him unclean.

But if the priest shall look, and, behold, there be no white hair in the sore, and it be not lower than the other skin, but be somewhat dark; then the priest shall shut him up seven days:

And on the seventh day the priest shall look upon the sore again: and, behold, if the sore be spread abroad in the skin, then the priest shall pronounce him unclean: it is a plague of leprosy.

**Yet if the rising be stubborn, and the priest shall see that the scab is not spread, but is hardened into a single mass, and its hue is as a ripe olive, verging upon a sickly yellow, then the priest shall command that they bring forth the man, and he shall be shut up yet another seven days.**

And it came to pass that there were certain sores that were not as the common leprosy, but were a grievous and deeper corruption, a swelling of the flesh that was filled with a vile humor.

**Concerning the Boil of Deep Corruption**

If, in the skin of his flesh, there arise a swelling that is not flat, but is raised up like a hillock, and its head be tender and glistening, the priest shall look upon it.

And the priest shall put forth his finger and press upon the edge of the swelling. And if, when pressed, the skin about it doth not spring back, but retaineth the mark of the finger, being pitted and dead, then it is a sign of a great uncleanliness.

And the priest shall look upon the color of the swelling. For if it be all of one color, a dull ashen grey, it may be a common boil. But if the center thereof shall turn to a fiery red, streaked with veins of purple and black, like unto a cluster of rotten grapes, this is the mark of the plague.

And if the priest shall take a clean linen cloth and lay it upon the sore, and when he removes it, the cloth shall be stained with a thin, watery ichor, and the sore beneath weepeth, then the man is unclean.

**But more grievous is the Boil that is full of corruption.**

And the priest shall look upon the boil, and he shall see that its head is not open, but sealed with a thin, yellow crust, like amber. And beneath that crust, the flesh doth quiver with a foulness within.

And the priest shall take a thorn of the acacia tree, and with the point thereof, he shall gently pierce the crust of the boil.

And if, when the crust is broken, there doth issue forth a thick, creamy puss, the color of curdled milk mixed with the dregs of wine, and it oozeth out slowly, like unto the slime of a snail, then this is a deep-seated filth.

And the priest shall take the two thumbs of his hands and press upon the base of the swelling, squeezing from the bottom upward.

And as he squeezeth, the puss shall come forth more violently, and its color shall be seen to be a deeper yellow, like unto the yolk of an egg, but shot through with strings of crimson blood. And the odor thereof shall be offensive, like that of a thing long dead, and it shall be a stench in the nostrils of the priest.

And the puss shall not be clear nor watery, but shall be ropy and thick, and it shall cling to the fingers of the priest. And when he wipeth it upon a potsherd, the stain thereof shall remain, a greasy and fetid mark, and shall not be washed away with water.

And the priest shall look into the hollow of the sore from whence the puss hath issued. And if he seeth within that hollow not clean flesh, but a raw and angry red, mixed with streaks of white, like the marbling of spoiled meat, and the bottom thereof is not level but ragged and torn, then the plague is not merely in the skin, but hath taken root in the flesh beneath.

Then the priest shall pronounce him utterly unclean. He shall rent his clothes, and the hair of his head shall be uncovered, and he shall put a covering upon his upper lip, and shall cry, Unclean, unclean.

All the days wherein the puss shall flow from the sore, or wherein the sore remaineth wet and weeping, he shall be unclean. He is unclean; he shall dwell alone; without the camp shall his habitation be.

And if the puss shall cease to flow, and the sore shall dry up, and a new, healthy scab, of a whitish-pink color, the color of the flesh of a young lamb, shall cover it over, then the priest shall look upon it. But even so, he shall wait seven days to see if the corruption reappear within.

##Parables
**Five Parables of the Kingdom of Heaven Within**

---

**Parable I: The Parable of the Sunlight and the Face**

The Kingdom of Heaven is like the light of the sun that falls upon the earth at the breaking of dawn.

Behold, two men stood upon a hilltop as the morning sun rose over the mountains. The first man was a philosopher, learned in the ways of nature, and he said, "This light is but the radiation of photons emitted from a sphere of burning gas at a distance of ninety-three million miles. Its warmth is the result of infrared waves striking my skin. It is heat, and it is measurable, and it is known."

The second man was a simple soul, a child of the fields, who had never studied the sciences. He opened his face to the light, and tears of joy welled in his eyes. He said, "The sun is the smile of God upon my face. Its warmth is the embrace of the Creator. It is love made visible, and it is a gift, and I am glad."

And the Lord said to the philosopher, "You have measured the heat, but you have not felt the warmth. You have calculated the distance, but you have not received the embrace. The Kingdom of Heaven is not known by measurement, but by reception. It is not a fact to be understood, but a presence to be felt."

For the sunlight is the same upon both men, yet one receives it as heat, and the other as joy. The warmth of the sun upon the face is not merely a physical sensation; it is a conscious experience, a gift of the moment, a blessing of the present. The Kingdom of Heaven is like that warmth: it is felt only by those who open their faces to it, who receive it with gratitude, who conceive it in happiness as a gift from the hand of the Father.

So it is with the Kingdom: it is not a place to be found, but a presence to be realized. It is not a future hope, but a present experience. It is the warmth of the Divine upon the soul, and only those who receive it as a gift shall know it. And those who receive it shall find that the Kingdom is within them, for the light that shines upon them is the light of their own awakened consciousness.

---

**Parable II: The Parable of the Seed and the Garden**

The Kingdom of Heaven is like a seed that a man planted in the garden of his own soul.

Now this seed was small, smaller than a grain of mustard, and it was hidden deep within the earth, unseen by any eye. And the man who planted it did not know what manner of seed it was, for it had been given to him by a stranger who passed by in the night.

And the stranger had said to him, "Take this seed, and plant it in the garden of your heart. Water it with your attention, warm it with your desire, and guard it with your silence. And when it grows, you shall know what it is."

So the man took the seed, and he planted it. And he watched over it with great care. In the morning, he would sit beside the spot where he had planted it, and he would focus his mind upon it. He would imagine the seed breaking open, the tiny root reaching down into the darkness, the slender shoot pushing up toward the light. He would feel the warmth of the sun upon the soil, and he would feel the moisture of the rain upon the earth.

And as he watched, the seed began to grow. First, there was a tiny green shoot, barely visible. Then there were leaves, and then a stem, and then branches. And the man cared for the plant, and he spoke to it, and he sang to it, and he loved it.

And one day, the plant bore fruit. And the fruit was unlike any fruit the man had ever seen. It was not an apple, nor a fig, nor a pomegranate. It was a fruit of light, a fruit of peace, a fruit of joy that filled the garden with its radiance.

And the man understood then what the stranger had said. The seed was the Kingdom of Heaven, planted in his own soul. The growth was his own awakening. The fruit was the conscious experience of the Divine in every moment.

And the Lord said, "The Kingdom of Heaven is like this seed. It is small and hidden, yet it contains within it all that shall come to be. It must be planted, watered, warmed, and guarded. It must be grown with attention and intention. For the Kingdom is not given to the passive, but to the active. It is not discovered by the wanderer, but cultivated by the gardener. Plant it within yourself, and water it with your thoughts, and you shall find that the Kingdom is within you, and that you are within the Kingdom."

---

**Parable III: The Parable of the Sparrow and the Markings**

The Kingdom of Heaven is like a little sparrow that alights upon a branch, and upon its back there are markings of speckled brown and white.

Behold, a man sat beneath a tree, and he was troubled in his heart. He was seeking the Kingdom of Heaven, and he had traveled to many temples, and he had read many books, and he had consulted many teachers. But the Kingdom eluded him. It was as a shadow that fled before him, as a whisper that he could never quite hear.

And as he sat in his despair, a little sparrow flew down and perched upon a branch before him. And the man looked upon the sparrow, and he noticed the markings upon its back. There were curves and there were jaggeds, there were spots and there were streaks, and they formed a pattern of great beauty and complexity.

And the man looked more closely, and he saw that the pattern upon the sparrow's back was like a map of a hidden land. There were rivers of brown and mountains of white, valleys of speckle and peaks of solid color. And the man was so taken by the pattern that he forgot his despair, and he gave his full attention to the sparrow.

And the sparrow, feeling the man's gaze, turned its head and looked at him. And in that instant, the man saw something else. He saw not only the pattern, but the bird. He saw the life within the markings, the consciousness behind the feathers, the soul of the creature that looked upon him with bright, unblinking eyes.

And a voice said to him, "You have searched for the Kingdom in temples and books, but the Kingdom is closer than your own breath. You have sought it in the future and the past, but it is here, in this moment, in this sparrow. The markings you see are but the outward sign. The bird itself is the reality. Look upon the sparrow, and know that the Kingdom is as close to you as this creature. It is not in the pattern, but in the noticing of the pattern. It is not in the bird, but in the union of your consciousness with the bird."

And the man understood. The Kingdom of Heaven is not a place to be found, but a way of seeing. It is the consciousness that sees the markings and the bird, the pattern and the life, the form and the spirit. It is the attention that perceives the infinite in the finite, the eternal in the moment.

And the Lord said, "Consider the sparrow. It does not sow, neither does it reap, nor gather into barns; yet your Heavenly Father feeds it. The markings upon its back are a testimony of His artistry. But the Kingdom is not in the markings; it is in the seeing. It is not in the bird; it is in the relationship. For the Kingdom of Heaven is the conscious awareness of the Divine in all things, the perception of the Creator in the creation, the recognition of the Self in the other."

---

**Parable IV: The Parable of the Disciplined Mind and the Notice of the Instant**

The Kingdom of Heaven is like a discipline of thought, a training of the attention, a schooling of the eye and the ear.

Behold, a master taught his students, saying, "The Kingdom of Heaven is hidden within you, but you cannot see it because your minds are filled with clamor. Your thoughts race like wild horses, and your attention leaps from this to that like a monkey in the trees. You are never fully present, never fully aware, never fully conscious."

And the master gave them a discipline. He said, "Sit quietly beneath this tree. Notice everything. Notice the left and the right, the above and the below, the near and the far. Notice the sound of the wind, the smell of the earth, the feel of the air upon your skin. And when a thought arises, do not chase it, do not fight it, do not cling to it. Simply notice it, and let it pass."

So the students sat, and they practiced the discipline. And as they practiced, their minds became quieter, more still, more attentive. And they began to notice things they had never noticed before. They noticed the curve of a leaf, the pattern of a shadow, the song of a bird, the dance of the light.

And one day, one of the students looked at a sparrow that alighted upon a branch, and he noticed the markings upon its back. He saw the curves and the jaggeds, the speckles and the streaks, and he saw them not as a blur, but as a distinct pattern. And in the very instant that he noticed the markings, he also noticed the bird itself. He noticed its life, its consciousness, its presence.

And the master said to him, "You have seen the Kingdom. For the Kingdom of Heaven is not in the bird, nor is it in the marking, nor is it in the noticing alone. It is in the instant of pure attention, when the mind is disciplined and the heart is open. It is in the moment when you are so fully present that you become one with what you perceive. The curves and the jaggeds are seen in an instant, and in that instant, you are the bird, and the bird is you, and the Kingdom is all."

For the Kingdom of Heaven is the state of pure consciousness, the awareness of what is, without the filter of desire or fear or judgment. It is the discipline of thought that brings the mind to rest, that opens the eye to the infinite, that reveals the Divine in the ordinary. And it is within you, waiting to be noticed, waiting to be known.

---

**Parable V: The Parable of the Many Sparrows and the One Seeing**

The Kingdom of Heaven is like a flock of sparrows that settle upon a field. Each bird is similar, each has markings of brown and white, each sings a song of chirping and trilling. But no two are identical.

Behold, a man looked upon a great flock of sparrows that covered a field. There were hundreds, perhaps thousands, and each one was speckled in a manner that was unique, yet similar. And the man said to his companion, "See all the birds! They are so many, and they are so alike. How can I tell one from another?"

And the companion said, "You cannot. They are all the same to you because you see them only in their multitude. But to the sparrow, each one is distinct. Each knows its own mate, its own nest, its own territory. They are not all the same; they are all unique. The sameness is in your seeing, not in their being."

And the man looked again, and he practiced the discipline of attention. He chose one sparrow, and he looked at it for a long time. He noticed its markings, the particular pattern of brown and white, the curve of the wing, the tilt of the head. He noticed its song, its movements, its way of hopping and pecking. And when he had truly seen that one sparrow, he turned to another, and he saw that it was different, unique, particular.

And the Lord said, "The Kingdom of Heaven is like this flock of sparrows. It is all around you, and it is hidden in plain sight. To the undisciplined eye, all the birds are the same, and the Kingdom is nowhere to be seen. To the disciplined mind, each bird is unique, and the Kingdom is everywhere. The difference is not in the birds, but in the seeing. The Kingdom is not a thing to be found; it is a way of looking. It is the discipline of attention that perceives the infinite variety of the world and the infinite unity of the Divine."

And the man understood that the Kingdom of Heaven is not a place, nor a destination, nor a reward. It is the conscious experience of reality as it is, the awareness of the presence of the Creator in every creature, the recognition of the Self in every other. It is the power of the mind to create the world as it is lived consciously, to see the pattern of the Divine in the speck of the sparrow, to feel the love of the Creator in the warmth of the sun, to know the Kingdom within as the Kingdom without.

For there are many sparrows, but there is one seeing. There are many markings, but there is one attention. There are many paths, but there is one journey. And the Kingdom of Heaven is the journey, the seeing, the attention. It is within you, and it is all around you. It is the smile of God upon your face, the warmth of the sun upon your skin, the song of the sparrow in your ear. It is now, it is here, and it is you.

# The Beggar at the Gate of Jericho
## A Story of the Kingdom Seen Through Dust and Tears

---

The sun hung over the Judean wilderness like a bronze shield, white-hot and merciless, casting no shadows at noon. The road from Jerusalem to Jericho wound downward through the rocky desolation, a serpent of dust and stone that descended nearly three thousand feet in the span of a day's journey. The air grew thicker, heavier, as one descended toward the Jordan Valley, and the heat rose from the limestone like the breath of a furnace.

Upon this road, in the shade of the city gate of Jericho, there sat a man named Baruch, which means "blessed," though he had never known a day of blessing in all his sixty years. He was a beggar, blind from birth, and his eyes were clouded with the white film of cataracts that had never known the light. His skin was the color of dried leather, cracked and weathered by decades of sun and wind. His beard was gray and matted, his hair thin upon his scalp, and his robes were nothing more than rags pieced together from discarded cloth—the faded blue of a garment that had once been fine, the brown of sackcloth, the dirty white of linen worn thin as tissue.

He sat upon a stone that had been worn smooth by generations of beggars before him, and before him he had spread a piece of goat's hair cloth upon the dust. In his hand, he held a clay bowl, chipped and cracked, its surface glazed with the grease of many hands. His fingers were gnarled with the arthritis of age, the knuckles swollen like knots upon a branch, and he trembled slightly from the ague that had plagued him since childhood.

Beside him, on a cord around his neck, hung a wooden cup that he would rattle to announce his presence, for he could not see the faces of those who passed, nor know when they approached. He would call out in a voice that had grown hoarse from years of pleading, a voice that cracked and faltered like a dry stream bed. And the cry that rose from his throat was the most ancient cry of humanity, the plea that has been uttered since the first mouth opened in hunger:

"Alms! Alms for the blind! Have mercy upon a blind man! May the God of Abraham bless you and your children for a hundred generations!"

And the people passed him by.

---

The merchants from the East passed him by, their camels laden with spices and silks, the bells upon their harnesses jingling with every step. They were men of substance, their robes of fine linen dyed with the crimson of the murex snail, their turbans white and spotless, their purses heavy with shekels. They heard his cry, and they saw his rags, and they turned their faces away as if they had seen a snake upon the path.

The tax collectors passed him by, men who had grown rich on the backs of the poor, who exacted their tithes with the edge of the sword. Their sandals were of fine leather, their cloaks heavy with embroidery, and they walked with the swagger of men who owed allegiance to no one but Rome. They heard his cry, and they laughed among themselves, and one of them tossed a single coin into the dust near the beggar's bowl, not in charity, but in contempt, so that Baruch had to grope for it with his blind fingers, scraping his nails against the stones.

The women of the city passed him by, carrying their water jars upon their heads, their veils drawn across their faces to shield them from the sun. They were busy with the work of life, with the grinding of grain and the drawing of water and the tending of children. They heard his cry, and they whispered among themselves, saying, "He is the one who was born blind, the curse of God upon him. It is the sin of his parents that has visited this upon him. We must not touch him, lest we be defiled."

And Baruch heard their whispers, and he knew the judgment that was upon him. He had heard it all his life—that he was cursed, that he was unclean, that he was less than human. He had been driven from the synagogue, forbidden to enter the Temple, relegated to the margins of society where the lepers and the lunatics dwelt. He had no family, no friends, no home. His only possession was the stone upon which he sat, and even that was not his own.

---

And then, at the hour when the sun was at its zenith and the dust hung thick as a curtain in the air, there came a commotion from the direction of the road. The crowd that had been flowing through the gate like a slow river suddenly parted, and the murmur of voices rose in excitement.

"It is He! It is the prophet from Nazareth! He is coming! The one who heals the sick and opens the eyes of the blind!"

Baruch's heart began to pound in his chest. He had heard the rumors, of course. Every beggar in Jericho had heard the rumors. A man from Galilee, a rabbi, a healer, a prophet who spoke of the Kingdom of God in parables and riddles. There were stories of lepers being cleansed, of paralytics walking, of demons being cast out with a word. There were even stories of the dead being raised.

But Baruch had never believed. He had seen too many false messiahs, too many charlatans who took the money of the desperate and gave nothing in return. He had learned that hope was the cruelest gift of all, for it promised what it could not deliver, and left the heart emptier than before.

Yet as the crowd drew nearer, something stirred within him. A warmth in his chest, a trembling in his hands, a whisper in his soul that he could not explain. And he cried out, louder than before, with a desperation he had not known he possessed:

"JESUS! JESUS, SON OF DAVID! HAVE MERCY ON ME!"

The crowd around him went silent. Someone hissed at him, "Be quiet! Do not trouble the Master!" Another voice, sharper and more contemptuous, said, "Hold your tongue, you blind beggar! You are not worthy to speak His name!"

But Baruch would not be silent. The warmth in his chest had become a fire, and the fire would not be quenched. He stood up, his body trembling, his hands reaching out blindly toward the sound of the approaching steps. He cried out again, his voice breaking with tears:

"JESUS, SON OF DAVID! HAVE MERCY ON ME! HAVE MERCY ON ME, A POOR, BLIND, WRETCHED SINNER!"

---

And the footsteps stopped.

Baruch heard the crowd fall silent, heard the shuffling of feet as people pressed back, heard the whisper of robes upon the dust. He felt the presence of someone standing before him, and the presence was different from anything he had ever felt. It was not the presence of a man, but the presence of something else, something that made the air itself vibrate, that made the sun seem brighter, that made the dust itself seem alive.

And then a voice, a voice that was gentle as the dove and yet powerful as the thunder, a voice that was as familiar as the voice of his father, though he had never seen his father's face, spoke to him.

"What do you want Me to do for you?"

Baruch fell to his knees, his hands grasping at the hem of a robe that he could not see, his tears falling upon the dust. "Lord," he said, "I want to receive my sight. I have been blind since birth. I have never seen the sun, nor the moon, nor the face of my mother. I have never seen the flowers of the field, nor the birds of the air, nor the mountains of my homeland. I have spent my life in darkness, and I have begged for bread, and I have been despised, and I am weary, Lord. I am so weary."

And the voice, which was both warm and terrible, both tender and absolute, said to him, "Your faith has healed you. Receive your sight."

And then the presence did something that Baruch had never expected. The hands of the teacher reached out, and they touched his clouded eyes. The fingers were rough, calloused from the labor of a carpenter, yet they were gentle, so gentle, as gentle as the touch of a mother upon her child.

And then, in an instant, the world exploded into light.

---

Baruch gasped, his breath catching in his throat. He opened his eyes, and for the first time in sixty years, he saw. He saw the blue of the sky, the gold of the sun, the green of the palm trees that lined the road. He saw the white limestone of the city walls, the red clay of the rooftops, the brown dust of the road. He saw the colors of the robes that surrounded him—the crimson of a merchant's cloak, the blue of a woman's veil, the white of a priest's turban.

And then he saw the face of the one who had healed him.

It was a face like no other. It was the face of a man, yet it was the face of something more. The skin was dark from the sun, the beard black and full, the eyes deep and wise and full of a love that Baruch had never seen before. There were lines upon the face, lines of sorrow and of joy, lines of pain and of hope, and in the eyes there were tears, tears that wept for the suffering of the world, tears that rejoiced at the healing of one blind man.

Baruch could not speak. His heart was full to bursting, and his tears would not stop. He reached out and touched the face of the healer, touching the cheek, the beard, the brow, to confirm what his eyes were telling him. And he saw the healer smile, a smile that was full of tenderness, a smile that said, "You are My beloved. You were never cursed. You were never abandoned. I have always been with you."

And the healer said to him, "Your faith has made you whole. Go in peace."

And then the healer turned and continued on His journey, and the crowd moved around Him, and Baruch was left kneeling in the dust.

---

Baruch rose to his feet, and he looked around him, and he saw the world with new eyes. He saw the sky and the trees and the birds and the walls. He saw the faces of the people who passed him by, and he saw something that he had never seen before. He saw their suffering. He saw the lines of worry upon the merchant's face, the weariness in the tax collector's eyes, the loneliness in the woman's heart. He saw the pain that everyone carried, the burdens that everyone bore, the darkness that everyone experienced.

And he understood. The healing was not only for himself. The healing was for everyone. The Kingdom of Heaven was not a place, but a way of seeing. It was the recognition that every human being was beloved, every soul was precious, every life was sacred. It was the knowledge that the blind man and the merchant, the tax collector and the priest, the woman and the child, were all children of the same Father, all deserving of the same mercy, all yearning for the same light.

And Baruch knew what he must do.

He took the clay bowl that he had used to beg, and he turned it over, and he emptied its coins upon the dust. He took the wooden cup that had hung around his neck, and he placed it upon the stone. He took his rags, the garments of his old life, and he put them aside.

And then he began to walk through the crowd, touching people, speaking to them, telling them what he had seen and heard and experienced. He found the merchant with the crimson cloak, and he said, "The Lord has healed me. Do not be afraid. His love is for you as well." He found the tax collector with the heavy purse, and he said, "The Lord has shown me mercy. He will show you mercy too." He found the women with their water jars, and he said, "The Lord has opened my eyes. He will open yours as well."

And the crowd saw him, and they were amazed. And many of them fell to their knees and wept, and they asked the Lord to open their own eyes, to heal their own blindness, to show them the Kingdom that was hidden in plain sight.

---

For the lesson of the beggar at the gate of Jericho is this:

The Kingdom of Heaven is not a reward for the worthy, but a gift for the desperate. It is not earned by works, but received by faith. It is not seen by the eyes of the body, but by the eyes of the soul.

The blind man was healed because he called out, not because he deserved it, not because he had earned it, but because he believed. He believed that the love of God was for him, even though he was a beggar, even though he was blind, even though he was despised. He believed that the mercy of God was greater than the judgment of men. He believed that the Kingdom of Heaven was not a future hope, but a present reality.

And the truth that Jesus taught, and the truth that the disciples were learning, and the truth that the crowd was beginning to understand, is that giving from the heart is the greatest medicine for the suffering of the world. It is not the size of the gift that matters, but the love behind it. It is not the worthiness of the recipient, but the compassion of the giver. It is not the logic of the world, but the foolishness of grace that heals the broken, that lifts the fallen, that opens the eyes of the blind.

And yes, the beggar provides no work. Yes, the poor are often a burden. Yes, there are easier ways to get money, quicker ways to gain profit, more efficient ways to run the world. But the Kingdom of Heaven is not about efficiency. It is not about profit. It is not about the logic of the world. It is about the love of God, which falls like rain upon the just and the unjust, which shines like sun upon the good and the evil, which flows like a river through the desert, bringing life to all who drink.

The beggar at the gate of Jericho teaches us that every human being is worthy of compassion. Every soul, no matter how broken, no matter how lost, no matter how despised, is a child of God. And when we give from the heart, when we treat the least among us with the same love we would show to the greatest, we are not only healing them. We are healing ourselves. We are opening our own eyes. We are entering the Kingdom of Heaven.

---

## The Lesson of the Disciples

And as Jesus continued on His journey, the disciples came to Him, and they asked Him privately, "Rabbi, why did You heal that beggar? He had never done a good work in his life. He had never contributed to the Temple, never given alms, never fasted or prayed. He was a burden upon the people, a drain upon the resources of the city. Why did You show him such mercy?"

And Jesus stopped, and He turned to them, and His eyes were full of sorrow, and He said:

"Truly, I say unto you, the Kingdom of Heaven is like a man who was walking through the wilderness, and he found a lost coin in the dust. And he rejoiced over the lost coin, and he said to his friends, 'Rejoice with me, for I have found that which was lost.' In the same way, the Kingdom of Heaven is like a shepherd who had a hundred sheep, and he lost one. And he left the ninety-nine and went into the wilderness to find the one that was lost. And he did not care that the ninety-nine were safe and sound. He cared that one was lost. And he searched until he found it, and he rejoiced, and he said to his friends, 'Rejoice with me, for I have found my sheep that was lost.'

"Truly, I say unto you, the Kingdom of Heaven is not for the righteous, but for the sinners. It is not for the healthy, but for the sick. It is not for those who are already whole, but for those who are broken. The beggar at the gate of Jericho was lost, and I have found him. He was blind, and I have given him sight. He was poor, and I have made him rich. He was despised, and I have called him beloved.

"Do you not understand? The mercy of God is not a reward, but a gift. The love of God is not earned, but received. The Kingdom of Heaven is not a place you enter by your own efforts, but a reality you experience by grace. And when you give from the heart, when you treat the poor and the broken and the despised as beloved children of God, you are not giving to them. You are giving to Me. For whatever you have done to the least of these My brethren, you have done it unto Me.

"So go, and do likewise. Do not judge the worth of the beggar. Do not measure the profit of the gift. Do not calculate the logic of the world. Simply give from the heart, and you shall find that your heart is opened, and your eyes are opened, and you shall see the Kingdom that is hidden in plain sight."

---

And the disciples were silent, for they understood that they had much to learn. They had traveled with Jesus for three years, and they had seen many miracles, and they had heard many parables, and they had learned many lessons, but they were still learning the greatest lesson of all:

The Kingdom of Heaven is within you. It is within every beggar, every blind man, every broken soul. It is within the dust and the sweat and the tears. It is within the sunlight that warms your face, the sparrow that sings in the tree, the kindness that you show to another. It is within you, and it is all around you, and it is waiting only for you to open your eyes and see.

---

## The Colors and Images of the Moment

And if you were there, if you had stood at the gate of Jericho on that day, you would have seen the dust rising like the incense of a thousand prayers, golden and thick and heavy with the scent of the earth. You would have seen the sun as a disc of white fire against the blue, the palm trees as black silhouettes against the heat, the city walls as a ribbon of cream and ochre that wound into the sky.

You would have seen the robes of the people, the crimson and the indigo, the saffron and the white, the patterns of embroidery that had been stitched by the hands of wives and mothers and daughters, the colors that spoke of wealth and poverty, of status and humility, of hope and despair.

You would have seen the tears upon the face of the blind man, clear as the water of the Jordan, falling like diamonds upon the dust. You would have seen the hands of Jesus, calloused and strong, gentle and loving, reaching out to touch the eyes that had never seen the light. You would have seen the light itself, the light that exploded from the moment of healing, the light that filled the eyes of the blind man for the first time, the light that was not the light of the sun but the light of the Kingdom, the light that is the glory of God.

And you would have heard the sound, the sound of the blind man's cry, the sound of his gasping breath, the sound of his weeping and his laughing and his shouting all at once. You would have heard the murmur of the crowd, the whispers of the people, the prayers that rose from the hearts of the desperate and the hopeful and the broken.

And you would have seen the man rise from his knees, and you would have seen him turn to the crowd, and you would have seen his face, the face of a man who had been reborn, the face of a man who had seen the Kingdom, the face of a man who would never be blind again.

And you would have been changed.

## A Prayer of the Healed Beggar

O Lord Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me.
I was blind, and You gave me sight.
I was poor, and You made me rich.
I was despised, and You called me beloved.

I am not worthy, Lord.
I am a beggar still, a poor and broken soul,
Clinging to the hem of Your garment,
Pleading for a crumb of Your mercy.

But You have given me more than crumbs.
You have given me the bread of life.
You have given me the water of life.
You have given me Your love.

Now open my eyes, Lord,
That I might see the needs of my neighbor.
Open my hands, Lord,
That I might give to the poor.
Open my heart, Lord,
That I might love as You have loved.

For I have received everything,
And I have nothing to give but what I have received.
But I give it freely, Lord,
As freely as You have given to me.

May the Kingdom of Heaven come,
And may I be its servant.
May the will of God be done,
And may I be its instrument.

Amen and Amen.
