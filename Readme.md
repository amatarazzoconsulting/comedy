**Leviticus 14**
**Concerning Further Signs of Impure Flesh, and the Examination of Deep-Seated Boils**

And the LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

When a man or a woman shall have in the skin of their body a rising, a scab, or a bright spot, and it be in the skin of their flesh like the plague of leprosy, then they shall be brought unto Aaron the priest, or unto one of his sons the priests.

And the priest shall look upon the sore in the skin of the flesh: and if the hair in the sore be turned white, and the sore in sight be deeper than the skin of his flesh, it is a plague of leprosy: and the priest shall look on him, and pronounce him unclean.

But if the priest shall look, and, behold, there be no white hair in the sore, and it be not lower than the other skin, but be somewhat dark; then the priest shall shut him up seven days:

And on the seventh day the priest shall look upon the sore again: and, behold, if the sore be spread abroad in the skin, then the priest shall pronounce him unclean: it is a plague of leprosy.

**Yet if the rising be stubborn, and the priest shall see that the scab is not spread, but is hardened into a single mass, and its hue is as a ripe olive, verging upon a sickly yellow, then the priest shall command that they bring forth the man, and he shall be shut up yet another seven days.**

And it came to pass that there were certain sores that were not as the common leprosy, but were a grievous and deeper corruption, a swelling of the flesh that was filled with a vile humor.

**Concerning the Boil of Deep Corruption**

If, in the skin of his flesh, there arise a swelling that is not flat, but is raised up like a hillock, and its head be tender and glistening, the priest shall look upon it.

And the priest shall put forth his finger and press upon the edge of the swelling. And if, when pressed, the skin about it doth not spring back, but retaineth the mark of the finger, being pitted and dead, then it is a sign of a great uncleanliness.

And the priest shall look upon the color of the swelling. For if it be all of one color, a dull ashen grey, it may be a common boil. But if the center thereof shall turn to a fiery red, streaked with veins of purple and black, like unto a cluster of rotten grapes, this is the mark of the plague.

And if the priest shall take a clean linen cloth and lay it upon the sore, and when he removes it, the cloth shall be stained with a thin, watery ichor, and the sore beneath weepeth, then the man is unclean.

**But more grievous is the Boil that is full of corruption.**

And the priest shall look upon the boil, and he shall see that its head is not open, but sealed with a thin, yellow crust, like amber. And beneath that crust, the flesh doth quiver with a foulness within.

And the priest shall take a thorn of the acacia tree, and with the point thereof, he shall gently pierce the crust of the boil.

And if, when the crust is broken, there doth issue forth a thick, creamy puss, the color of curdled milk mixed with the dregs of wine, and it oozeth out slowly, like unto the slime of a snail, then this is a deep-seated filth.

And the priest shall take the two thumbs of his hands and press upon the base of the swelling, squeezing from the bottom upward.

And as he squeezeth, the puss shall come forth more violently, and its color shall be seen to be a deeper yellow, like unto the yolk of an egg, but shot through with strings of crimson blood. And the odor thereof shall be offensive, like that of a thing long dead, and it shall be a stench in the nostrils of the priest.

And the puss shall not be clear nor watery, but shall be ropy and thick, and it shall cling to the fingers of the priest. And when he wipeth it upon a potsherd, the stain thereof shall remain, a greasy and fetid mark, and shall not be washed away with water.

And the priest shall look into the hollow of the sore from whence the puss hath issued. And if he seeth within that hollow not clean flesh, but a raw and angry red, mixed with streaks of white, like the marbling of spoiled meat, and the bottom thereof is not level but ragged and torn, then the plague is not merely in the skin, but hath taken root in the flesh beneath.

Then the priest shall pronounce him utterly unclean. He shall rent his clothes, and the hair of his head shall be uncovered, and he shall put a covering upon his upper lip, and shall cry, Unclean, unclean.

All the days wherein the puss shall flow from the sore, or wherein the sore remaineth wet and weeping, he shall be unclean. He is unclean; he shall dwell alone; without the camp shall his habitation be.

And if the puss shall cease to flow, and the sore shall dry up, and a new, healthy scab, of a whitish-pink color, the color of the flesh of a young lamb, shall cover it over, then the priest shall look upon it. But even so, he shall wait seven days to see if the corruption reappear within.

For the plague of the boil is deceitful, and its puss is a testimony of the wickedness that dwelleth within the flesh. And the LORD spake all these things, that the children of Israel might know the clean from the unclean, and that they might purge the evil from among them.
